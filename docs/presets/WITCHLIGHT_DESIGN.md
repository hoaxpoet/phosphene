## 1. Source and what we are honouring

### 1.1 What the source is

Read from the Butterchurn-form source end to end, and from Matt's render (60 extracted GIF frames, stepped through individually; stills `00` and `12` in the reference folder are two of them).

Conceptually — no source expression, variable name or constant is reproduced here or anywhere in the repo (D-116 bullets 1–2):

The source keeps a CPU-side ring buffer of about a thousand 3-D points. Each frame it appends a few points at the head, generated by three damped sinusoidal oscillators whose frequencies and phases are re-rolled from random numbers on each detected beat — with a second set re-rolled every eighth beat and a third every sixty-fourth. The whole cloud is rotated slightly in 3-D each frame about a slowly drifting axis, then run through a neighbour-averaging relaxation pass that turns the raw scribble into a smooth calligraphic stroke. The buffer is drawn twice, once as a thin line and once as ~1024 alpha-graded round sprites, so it reads as a beaded ribbon fading toward its tail. Each point carries a colour taken from the normalised random vector that was live when it was emitted, so the ribbon is banded in arbitrary hues. The feedback warp is a no-op; trail persistence is frame decay alone. The composite adds three parallax layers of procedural stars scrolling in depth, a soft violet nebular glow, a term that dims the stars behind the ribbon, and a six-spoke lens flare at the head whose brightness is driven by mid-band energy above a threshold and whose colour is the head point's colour.

**It is gorgeous, and the shape of the curve means nothing.** It is a random walk wearing a beat as a metronome. Play the same track twice and you get two different figures; play two different tracks and you get the same species of figure. That is the weakness Witchlight exists to fix — and, conveniently, it is also the D-121 divergence axis.

### 1.2 Take / leave / transform

Ten concrete traits, each with a verdict.

| # | Trait in the source | Verdict | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Beaded luminous ribbon** — a thin line plus a run of alpha-graded round sprites, so the stroke reads as beads on a thread rather than a drawn curve | **TAKE** | This is the register, and it is what the light-painting references (`01`, `02`) show real burning trails actually doing. It is also what makes the trail's age legible at a glance. |
| 2 | **Neighbour-averaging relaxation over the point buffer** | **TAKE (structurally)** | The one mechanism worth taking as a *mechanism*, because it is what separates `01` from `10`. Re-expressed against Phosphene primitives per D-116 bullets 1–2; see §3 (design gated on §2 sign-off). |
| 3 | **Deep-space ground: near-black field with sparse parallax stars in three depth layers** | **TAKE** | Register. Our version reads star density and true-black level from `07` rather than from the source. |
| 4 | **Soft violet nebular bloom, offset from centre** | **TAKE** | Register, and it is what keeps the frame from being a black rectangle with a squiggle in it. Hue family and spatial frequency read from `06`. |
| 5 | **A bright point at the head of the stroke** | **TAKE, transform the bounds** | A burning trail must have a visible source; `03` is what one actually looks like at scale. What we do *not* take is its magnitude — see #9. |
| 6 | **Stars dimmed behind the ribbon** | **TAKE** | A cheap, correct depth cue: it reads as the ribbon occluding the field. Costs nothing and prevents the stroke from looking pasted on. |
| 7 | **Small-in-frame composition, figure tumbling in deep space** | **TAKE** | It is what makes the ribbon read as *hanging in the dark* rather than as a graphic. Composition is explicitly not our divergence axis (see §1.3), so we keep it. |
| 8 | **The path — three damped oscillators re-seeded from random numbers on beats** | **LEAVE** | The source's actual weakness, and the D-121 divergence axis. Replaced wholesale in §3 by a path driven from the track's harmonic and spectral state. |
| 9 | **Head flare — six-spoke lens flare, mid-band above a fixed threshold, unbounded magnitude, re-fires every hit** | **LEAVE the drive and the magnitude; TRANSFORM the form** | Three separate faults: (a) the flare saturates to near-full-frame white and erases the subject (`12`) — a fidelity failure before it is a safety one; (b) it re-fires on every hit with no refractory period, which is a photosensitivity failure Phosphene's gates will not pass; (c) it is driven by an *absolute threshold on an AGC-normalized band*, which is FA #31 / D-026 — the same kick reads differently across tracks. Witchlight keeps a bounded head burst (§5) driven from a deviation primitive. |
| 10 | **Per-segment hue from an arbitrary random unit vector** | **LEAVE** | The banding is pretty and carries nothing. Consequential half of the divergence axis: a bead's hue records the harmonic position at the moment it was laid down, so the ribbon's colour banding *is* the track's colour history. |

Two things the source does that are worth naming as **explicitly not ours**, because they are the shape a careless WL.2 would drift into:

- **Feedback warp as a no-op.** The source gets its trail from frame decay alone. That is a legitimate choice and we should not "improve" it by adding `mv_warp` — a warp would smear the beads and destroy trait #1. If WL.2 finds itself reaching for `mv_warp`, that is a signal the trail-persistence design in §3 is wrong, not that the preset needs a warp.
- **Nothing in the source is a fidelity target.** Per D-188 / the `preset-session` skill: the reference set is the look authority, and the musical role is the concept. `00` is committed as a register anchor and a D-121 baseline, not as a frame to match.

### 1.3 The divergence axis (D-121), stated operatively

> **Witchlight diverges from its source on the dominant motion model, and consequentially on palette character.** The pen tip's path is a function of the track's harmonic and spectral motion rather than of re-seeded random numbers: chord changes turn the stroke, so the figure hanging in the dark is a drawing of the last thirty seconds of the song. Colour follows from the same source — each bead's hue records where the harmony was when that bead was laid down, so the ribbon reads as the track's colour history rather than as an arbitrary rainbow. Same register as the source (same beaded luminous line, same dark sky, same violet bloom, same bright head), different motion, and the difference is trivially demonstrable side by side: the source's figure is the same species of scribble on any track and a *different* scribble on a repeat play; Witchlight's is different on every track and the same on a repeat play.

Decided by Matt, 2026-07-31. Rationale, and why not the two alternatives (a palette-and-sky swap; a large-and-close recomposition), is recorded in D-209. Both alternatives leave the stroke saying nothing about the music, so the musical-role gate — the hardest gate for this concept, and the one that killed Drift Motes (D-102) and Glass Brutalist (D-186) — stays weak under either.

**Risk, stated plainly:** no published demo draws a light-painting stroke from harmonic state. This is a level-3 mechanism on the grounding ladder and §6 will say so. §2 is the gate that decides whether the axis is founded at all.

### 2.2 The two traps the prompt named — both confirmed

**`spectral_centroid` reads ≈ 0.04–0.21 on real music, not 0–1.** Measured p5–p95 bands: 0.074–0.209 (so_what), 0.041–0.079 (there_there), 0.048–0.128 (love_rehab), 0.038–0.206 (live). The single 0.744 maximum on the live capture is an isolated excursion, not a working range. A `centroid × N` mapping moves less than one step across a whole track — the BUG-027 / CR.1.1 trap, now measured a third time. **Any Witchlight mapping on this primitive is written against the measured p5/p95 band, and WL.2 records the band it used.**

**`vocalsPitchConfidence` is not eligible as a hero driver.** It is *not* the historical all-zero case any more — it reaches 0.99 and is nonzero on 41.8 % of frames on so_what — but on the 89-minute live playlist it is nonzero on **4.5 %** of frames, and on 2 of the 3 offline fixtures it sits at 2–3 %. A driver that is silent 95 % of the time cannot carry a visual layer. Garnish only, if at all.

### 2.3 Findings the measurement produced that the prompt did not anticipate

These change the design and are the reason §§3–7 are gated on this table rather than written alongside it.

1. **`pulse_amp01` is dead.** Pinned at 1.000 from p5 to max across all four captures, including 318 383 live frames. It has no dynamic range and cannot drive anything. Struck from the candidate list.

2. **`harmonic_flux` and `tonal_tension` are alive live but near-dead on 2 of 3 offline fixtures.** On the live capture `harmonic_flux` has p50 0.058 / p95 0.081 and is essentially always nonzero; on love_rehab it is nonzero on 0.2 % of frames with a maximum of 0.0009. The natural design for "each chord change turns the stroke" wants `harmonic_flux` as an explicit chord-change detector — **and that is not available**, at least not from the offline fixture path, which is exactly the path `RouteCoverageTests` replays (cf. QG.1.1: offline fixtures cannot reach CPU-derived `StemFeatures`). A chord change therefore has to be read out of the *motion of the smoothed fifths phase itself*, not out of a flux primitive. Whether the offline/live gap is a fixture-generation artifact or a real capability gap is an open question WL.2 must not paper over.

3. **`tonal_phase_fifths` is alive on all four captures — the divergence axis is founded — but its angular rate varies ~10× between tracks.** Full ±π range everywhere. Raw, it is far too noisy to steer a pen (median per-frame step 0.31–1.04 rad), so it needs the circular EMA that CR.1.2 already established (smooth sin/cos, recombine via `atan2` — never the raw ±π sawtooth). Smoothed, here is what it actually does:

   | τ | so_what | there_there | love_rehab | live |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 0.35 s | 4.16 rad/s p95, 151 turns/min | 3.29, 113 | 24.9, 185 | 10.3, 154 |
   | 0.80 s | 1.72 rad/s p95, 62 turns/min | 1.48, 44 | 15.9, 163 | 4.62, 101 |
   | **1.50 s** | **0.91 rad/s p95, 2.0 turns/min** | **0.79, 2.0** | **11.4, 129** | **2.42, 48** |
   | 3.00 s | 0.45 rad/s p95, 0 turns/min | 0.39, 0 | 3.08, 56 | 1.18, 14 |

   (A "turn" = a sustained sign reversal in the smoothed angular velocity over ≥ 0.25 s. Total wrapped path over 30 s at τ = 1.5 s: 2.1 / 1.7 / 15.4 circles for the three fixtures.)

   At the τ = 1.5 s that CR.1.2 settled on, so_what and there_there give a slow, almost monotonic drift with essentially no reversals — a gentle, legible curve. love_rehab at the same τ is still travelling fifteen times around the circle in thirty seconds with 129 reversals per minute. **Handed to a pen tip unchanged, the same code draws reference `01` on one track and anti-reference `10` on the next.** This is the single most consequential measurement in this section: the harmonic driver is alive, but a smoothing time constant alone does not make it usable. §3 must specify rate governance — a velocity clamp or an arc-length-parameterised advance — as a first-class mechanism, not a tuning constant. It is also why mandatory trait #1 in the reference README is written as a mechanism requirement rather than a look requirement.

### 2.4 Gate verdict

**The harmonic drivers measure alive on four real captures of different character, so the D-121 divergence axis in §1.3 is founded and the fallback scope (palette-and-sky) is not triggered.** The axis carries two qualifications that §3 must design against rather than tune around: the ~10× cross-track rate spread on the hero driver (§2.3 item 3), and the absence of a usable offline chord-change primitive (§2.3 item 2).
