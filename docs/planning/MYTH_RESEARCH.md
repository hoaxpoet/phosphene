# Myth & Folklore Research — Sound Answered in Light

*2026-08-09 · Naming sprint round 4: myths, legends, and folk histories that resonate with the mission*

## The filter

Generic light gods and generic music gods are everywhere and mean nothing. The filter applied here: traditions where **sound and light interlock** — music that summons light, light that listens, performances that bring brightness back into a dark world. That interlock is the product: it listens to music and answers in light.

## The myths that resonate

**Ama-no-Iwato — the performance that brought back the sun** (Japan, *Kojiki*). Amaterasu seals herself in the heavenly rock-cave and the world goes dark. The wisdom-kami **Omoikane** designs the plan — roosters gathered to crow dawn-sound at a world with no dawn, a mirror hung to catch light that doesn't yet exist — and **Ame-no-Uzume** overturns a tub and stamps a drumming rhythm on it, dancing until the eight hundred myriad gods roar with laughter and heaven shakes. Amaterasu, unable to fathom music and joy in a lightless world, cracks the door; the mirror catches her radiance; the door is hauled open and light returns forever. This is the mythic origin of *kagura*, Japan's sacred performance art — and it is the mission stated as a myth: **the world is dark until someone performs, and the performance is what brings the light out.** Uzume's epithets: goddess of dawn, mirth, and revelry; patroness of performers. And the structure maps eerily well — Omoikane plans the session, Uzume performs it. An orchestrator and a renderer, cave-side, ~1,300 years ago.

**Hotaru koi — singing the light to you** (Japan, folk). A find that bears directly on the current working name: the children's warabe-uta *"Ho, ho, hotaru koi"* is sung specifically **to summon fireflies** — "come, firefly: the water over there is bitter, the water here is sweet." Firefly-light is folk-lured *by voice*. With the Obon association of fireflies as returning souls, the song is an invitation sung to living light. **This means the hotaru tradition contains the sound→light interlock natively** — Hotarugari isn't just "watching small lights perform," it's a tradition where you *sing* and the light *comes*. The current working name is stronger than we knew.

**Tansen and Raga Deepak — the voice that lit the lamps** (Mughal India, 16th-c. legend). Tansen, court musician to Akbar and one of the nine jewels, is commanded to sing Raga Deepak, the lamp raga. As he sings, the palace's unlit lamps ignite one by one — and the fire overtakes him, his body burning from the inside until singers answer with Raga Megh Malhar, the rain raga, and the sky opens. **A human voice literally producing light — song as ignition.** Folk legend about a historical, secular musician (naming an app for him is like naming one Paganini), retold freely across India for five centuries.

**The aurora that listens** (Sámi, Inuit, Finnish). To the Sámi, the northern lights are presence, not weather — and the core taboo is acoustic: do not whistle or sing at the lights, or they will notice you and sweep down. In Alaskan Inuit tradition the aurora (*aqsarniit*, "the football players" — spirits playing kickball with a walrus skull) is audible — whistling, crackling — and **responsive: whistle and it comes closer.** The Finnish half of the complex is *revontulet*, "fox fires": a firefox races the fells flinging sparks skyward with its tail. And in the Kalevala, Väinämöinen plays the kantele so beautifully that **the Moon leaves her station to settle in a birch and the Sun climbs down from his castle to listen** — until Louhi locks them in a mountain and the world goes dark. Light that responds to sound in real time is the app's exact behavior; these are the only traditions found where the audience performs *at* the light and it answers. (The words themselves — *guovssahas*, *aqsarniit* — stay where they belong; taking them would be extractive. The concept, though, is perfect copy.)

**Gandharva-nagara — the mirage-city of the celestial musicians** (Sanskrit, Hindu/Buddhist). Gandharvas are the celestial musicians of Indra's court. *Gandharva-nagara* — "city of the gandharvas" — is the standard Sanskrit term for a phantom city shimmering in the sky (a Fata Morgana), used by Buddhist philosophers as a classic simile for illusion: a city of light visible at sunrise that thins as the sun climbs. **A whole civilization saw architecture made of pure light and named it after musicians** — the assumption baked into the language being that if a city of light appears from nowhere, musicians built it. Conceptually the best match for an AI that architects a light-city per track; as a *name*, "Gandharva" fails the hear-it-once spelling test and belongs to gamers now (Genshin Impact location).

**The Colossus of Memnon — light answered in music** (Egypt/Greco-Roman, historically attested). After an earthquake cracked it in 27 BC, the colossus at Thebes sang: each dawn, first sunlight heating the stone drew out a musical tone. Romans crossed the empire to hear Memnon greet his mother Eos, the Dawn; emperors carved graffiti recording whether the god spoke for them. **The app's mission run backwards — light striking matter and producing music.** (As a name it's dead: memnon.app is a live product explicitly using the same myth, and Sony's Memnon owns the word in professional audio.)

**How music came to the world** (Aztec, *Histoyre du Mechique* tradition). The earth has no music. The wind god crosses a bridge of whales to the House of the Sun, where the Sun hoards all the musicians — flute players in gold, minstrels in blue, lullaby singers in white, love-singers in red. The wind storms the house; the musicians flee into his arms and are carried down to earth, where birds, streams, and people learn to sing. **Music's original home is the light — every song on earth is escaped sunlight, color-coded.** No usable name (the Nahuatl fails the spelling bar), but as an about-page story for a music visualizer it's unbeatable: the app just walks the music back home.

**Leigong and Dianmu — the percussion-and-light duo** (China). The thunder god beats a ring of drums; his wife, Mother of Lightning, flashes mirrors across the sky — canonically *so he can see his target before the drum falls*. In her origin legend she is a wrongly-executed girl, resurrected and armed with mirrors so no innocent is ever struck blind again. **Every drumbeat cued by a flash: a married rhythm section of light and sound.**

**The Western esoteric lineage — the sky as instrument.** Pythagoras hears the cosmos as tuned intervals; Kepler's *Harmonices Mundi* (1619) derives actual polyphony from planetary velocities; Fludd's celestial monochord stretches one string from Earth to heaven, tuned by a hand from a cloud. The visible lights of the sky *are* an instrument; their motion *is* the music — the intellectual charter for an orchestrator that plans light like a concert. And Coleridge's "The Eolian Harp" (1795), on the instrument played by no human hand, contains the mission statement verbatim, 230 years early: ***"A light in sound, a sound-like power in light."*** Public domain. That line belongs on the website regardless of what the app is called.

**Honored and left alone.** Shango answering the consecrated bata drums in lightning (Yoruba) — a perfect interlock, but Shango is actively worshipped in Yoruba religion, Santería, and Candomblé; not usable. Australian songlines — the deepest sound-creates-world tradition there is, and closed, clan-owned sacred knowledge; not usable, full stop. Kokopelli — the pop icon is a misattribution of a Hopi kachina with no light or flute association, already resented as commodified; not usable and not even the right myth.

## The real decision

Three names survive with full stories behind them:

**Hotarugari** — the incumbent, and this round strengthened it: the *hotaru koi* song means the tradition already contains "sing, and the light comes." Its domains are the cleanest of any name found in four rounds of vetting (the .com itself is free — rare luck), its collisions are nil. Its cost was and remains the five syllables.

**Uzume** — the challenger this round produced. The Iwato myth is the single best myth-product fit found in any tradition: darkness, then a *planned* performance (Omoikane the orchestrator, Uzume the performer), then light returned. Three syllables, singsong, same vowel-ended Japanese register as Hotarugari. Costs: uzume.com is taken, the .app needs registrar confirmation, and she is an actively venerated kami — low risk given the app honors her exact domain, but it's a judgment call Hotarugari (a practice, not a person) doesn't require. Irresistible detail either way: **Omoikane is a ready-made codename for the AI orchestrator subsystem**, whichever product name wins.

**Tansen** — the pragmatist's pick. Two syllables, spellable on first hearing, zero sacredness risk, and a five-century-old legend pointing the right direction (song ignites light). Costs: it reads as a person's name (because it is one — a revered one in India, where the app will have users), the .com is taken, and the story, while great, is about *lamps lighting* rather than *a session performed* — a slightly narrower fit than Iwato's full orchestrated arc.

## Copy vault (keep regardless of the name)

Coleridge, 1795: *"A light in sound, a sound-like power in light"* — the tagline, public domain. The Sámi/Inuit aurora: light you must not whistle at, because it listens — the about-page opener. The Aztec House of the Sun: all music is escaped sunlight, and every session walks it home. Kepler and Fludd: the sky was always an instrument. And whatever the app is called, the orchestrator has a name waiting: Omoikane, the kami who planned the performance that brought back the sun.
