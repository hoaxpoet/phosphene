DS.4 · design pass

The wait is the overture,
not a progress bar.

Two directions for the preparation screen, built from what the code already computes and what the brand already sanctions. Watch both, then pick a lane — this is the decision that unblocks the DS.4 prompt.

What preparation is today

A file-transfer dialog. Header “Preparing your session”, a progress bar, and a list of rows reading Waiting → Finding preview → Downloading → Separating stems → Caching → Ready. It reports on machinery. Nothing on screen suggests a performance is about to begin.

Meanwhile, out of sight, Uzume is listening to every track — deriving tempo, musical key, emotional mood, brightness, section count, and a full four-stem energy balance from each 30-second preview. All of it is computed, cached, handed to the Orchestrator, and never shown to you.

Direction A — The opening widens

The brand identity, made literal and made functional. “Two shadows nearly meet, leaving a tiny irregular opening through which prismatic light enters the dark. Darkness is the condition, the opening is the event, and colour is the consequence.” Every track Uzume finishes hearing sends one shaft of light through the seam, coloured by what it heard. Progress is the aperture — there is no progress bar, because the widening opening is the progress bar.

Hue = mood quadrant · height = energy · brightness = spectral centroid.

Direction B — Uzume is listening

Same information, opposite posture. The list stays, but stops reporting machinery and starts reporting discoveries. As the analysis cursor reaches each track it says what it just heard — 174 BPM, D♭ major, brushed drums leading. The wait becomes a readout of your own playlist that you did not know you wanted.

Listening to your playlist 0 of 8 heard
 
Metadata appears in the order the pipeline actually derives it: stems → MIR → beat grid.

The constraint that shapes both

The surprise model is absolute, and it is a design-system rule, not a preference: “Never exposes upcoming content.” The plan-preview views are already marked for removal for violating it. So the obvious entertainment — “here’s the amazing session we planned!” — is off the table.

The line that makes both directions legal: they reveal what Uzume heard in music you already chose, never what Uzume will do with it.

Showing thisVerdict
Tempo, key, mood, stem balance of a track you picked allowed Analysis of known input. The brand explicitly grants the full spectrum to “meaningful metadata”.
Which preset each track will get forbidden Upcoming choreography — the exact thing the surprise model protects.
The emotional arc across the session forbidden Same reason; this is what `PlanPreviewView` did and why it is being removed.
How many tracks are ready, and that you can start allowed Session state, not content. Already shipped.

What each direction costs

A — the opening

Shows off your music. Your playlist becomes light. Anticipation builds structurally: the aperture is visibly approaching a threshold.

Risk: abstract. If someone wants to know what is happening, it does not say. It could read as a fancy spinner.

B — the listening

Shows off Uzume’s ear. This is the moment that proves Uzume is not a visualiser — it demonstrably understands the music.

Risk: reads as a dashboard. Uzume already has a debug-overlay aesthetic to stay clear of, and this is a lot of monospace during what should be a held breath.

The one thing both need that does not exist yet

TrackPreparationStatus carries only the stage — .analyzing(stage:), .ready. The BPM, key, mood and stem balance are computed and cached but never published to the UI. Either direction needs the profile surfaced alongside the status. That is a small, contained engine change, and it is the real prerequisite the DS.4 prompt has to name.
