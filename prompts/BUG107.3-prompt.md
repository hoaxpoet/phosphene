# Session prompt — BUG107.3

## Increment BUG107.3 — how far does a 30-second grid drift, and on how much music?

**Type:** Engine instrumentation + diagnosis (`dsp.beat`). **Measurement only.** No change to
`BeatGridResolver`, `BeatActivationDecoder`, `DefaultBeatGridAnalyzer`'s decoding, or any playback
path. Nothing is made to analyse more audio in this increment.

**Objective.** After this session there is a measured answer to two questions that every proposed
fix for BUG-107 currently guesses at: **(1) how much does tempo actually move within a track, across
a real corpus rather than one anecdote, and (2) how badly does a grid derived from the first 30 s
mis-predict beat times later in the same track?** The deliverable is a report ending in ranked
options for closing BUG-107, each with the evidence that supports it — not an implementation.

**Why measurement only, again.** BUG-107 is root-caused (`BeatThisModel.tMax = 1500` frames at
50 fps = 30.0 s) and the obvious fix is **already built and already measured to make things worse**:
`BeatThisTiledInference.predictFullTrack` (behind `UZUME_FULLTRACK_DECODE`) decodes the whole track,
and FT.4.1 found *the tiler was the whole regression* — it moved bleed's grid 115.00 → 123.62
against a truth of 114.67, and it was what destroyed `BarLineEstimator`'s scores in FT.4's bundled
arm. So the question is not "should we analyse the whole track" — that was tried. The question is
how much of the track is actually wrong, and for what fraction of real music, because that decides
whether the fix is a grid warp, a drift correction, a re-decode, or nothing at all.

**D-206 is the reason this increment exists in this shape.** Phase TRK is parked and "must not
reopen without a changed premise about the **grid**, not the tracker." The 30 s window *is* a grid
premise. This increment produces the evidence that either changes it or confirms it — and if the
answer is "tempo barely moves on the material Uzume actually plays", that is a real finding and
BUG-107 gets downgraded rather than fixed.

## Settled facts carried into this increment

**The window is 30.0 s exactly.** `BeatThisModel.tMax = 1500`, frame rate fixed at 50.0 fps
(22050/441). Confirmed independently from the outside at PREP.1: across 11 tracks spanning
113–384 s, `beat_grid` stage cost correlates with track length at **r = −0.02** where `mir` is
**r = +0.96** (`docs/diagnostics/PREP1/runA-release-serial.csv`).

**One constant tempo is emitted for the whole file.** BUG-107's worked example: money accelerates
~120 → ~140 → ~130 BPM across the track and the analyzer emits a single tempo.

**Beats are the layer that works.** F 0.97–0.99 on the tracks where it is measured. Whatever this
increment recommends, it must not put that at risk — FT.4.1's finding is that touching the decode
is how you lose it.

**The live path is not naked.** `LiveBeatDriftTracker` corrects the cached grid against live audio
during playback, seeded by `CachedTrackData.gridOnsetOffsetMs`. So the question is not "does the
30 s grid alone carry the whole track" but "how much residual is left after the live tracker", and
the offline half is what this increment can measure.

**Ground truth exists and is trustworthy again.** BUG-102 is resolved; both disputed references were
re-annotated and arbitrated (BUG102.1/.2), so BeatBench is citable. Some fixtures are tap-provenance
and some are corpus rips — `GroundTruthStore.tapProvenance` says which, and cross-master comparisons
are unsound.

## Numbered tasks

1. **Capture the five-suite BeatBench baseline.** Current `main`, both env flags OFF, all five
   suites. This is the reference every later number is quoted against, and the guard that this
   increment changed nothing.
   **Done-when:** a baseline table committed under `docs/diagnostics/`, with the fixture set and
   provenance stated per track.

2. **Measure how much tempo actually moves, within tracks.** For every truthed fixture, derive a
   per-track tempo *trajectory* from the ground truth itself — inter-beat intervals over time, not a
   model output — and report the spread: median BPM, p05→p95, max local deviation from the
   first-30 s median, and where in the track it occurs. Ground truth first, because a model-derived
   trajectory would be measuring the thing under test.
   **Done-when:** a per-track table of tempo excursion, and a one-line answer to "on how many of
   these tracks does tempo move enough to matter."

3. **Quantify the mis-prediction directly.** For each track, take the grid the shipping path
   produces (30 s window) and score it against the truth **as a function of position in the track** —
   the first 30 s, then successive windows to the end. This is the number BUG-107 is actually about:
   not "is the tempo wrong" but "how far has the grid walked off the music by minute three."
   **Done-when:** a per-track curve of beat error vs. elapsed time, and the median time-to-first
   sustained loss of lock across the set. State it in **milliseconds and in beats**, because a
   preset firing an accent cares about the former and a bar clock about the latter.

4. **Extend the measurement past the truthed set.** Nine tracks decide nothing about a listener's
   library. Use the CENSUS corpus harness over a sample large enough to be worth quoting (state the
   sample and how it was drawn) with the ground-truth-free proxy from task 2 — IBI spread from the
   shipping grid is not usable here, so state plainly what the proxy is and what it cannot see.
   **Done-when:** a distribution of tempo excursion across the corpus sample, with the fraction of
   tracks above whatever threshold task 3 shows to matter.

5. **Establish what the live tracker already recovers.** The offline grid is an initialisation. Take
   one recorded session per available source and measure the residual drift *after*
   `LiveBeatDriftTracker` — `features.csv` carries `drift_ms` and `lock_state`. A fix aimed at the
   offline grid is worth much less if the live tracker already absorbs the error.
   **Done-when:** residual drift p50/p90/p99 for at least one tempo-moving track, quoted beside
   task 3's offline curve for the same track.

6. **Write the report.** `docs/diagnostics/BUG107_GRID_DRIFT_<date>.md`: the four measurements, then
   — separately and last — **options**, ranked by measured payoff, each stating what it risks. The
   options must include **"do nothing and downgrade BUG-107"** as a first-class entry if the numbers
   support it. Any option that re-decodes audio must state explicitly how it avoids FT.4.1's
   finding; "use the tiler" without that is not an option, it is a repeat.
   **Done-when:** every claim traces to a named measurement, and the beat layer's F/Cemgil/CMLt/AMLt
   are shown unchanged from task 1.

7. **HARD STOP — present to Matt.** The report, the headline number, the options. **Present, stop,
   and report.** No fix is implemented in this increment, however obvious it looks by then.
   **Done-when:** Matt has the report and has picked a direction.

## Do NOT

- **Do not change the decode.** No `tMax` edit, no tiler enablement, no `BeatGridResolver` /
  `BeatActivationDecoder` / `DefaultBeatGridAnalyzer` behavioural edit. This increment measures.
- **Do not re-run the tiler as the answer.** FT.1 (no odd meter recovered, bohemian regressed) and
  FT.4.1 (bleed 115.00 → 123.62 against a truth of 114.67) already measured it. A proposal built on
  it must first explain those two results.
- **Do not reopen phase TRK.** D-206 parked it, and the reopening condition is a changed premise
  about the grid — which is this increment's *output*, not its licence.
- **Do not wire `BarLineEstimator`, and do not propose wiring it.** Rejected on product grounds
  2026-09-04 (see §DECISION) — it declines on 7 of 9 tracks, and a decline is *no bars*, which takes
  the downbeat push away from Nacre and Glaze on most music. Bundling it would also make this
  increment's beat-layer no-regression claim unreadable — the exact mistake FT.4 made and FT.4.1 had
  to unpick.
- **Do not quote a metric that has not passed `--self-test`,** and do not compare a tap-provenance
  fixture against a corpus rip.
- **Do not file new `BUG-*` IDs** for "the grid is a 30 s window" — that is BUG-107. File one only
  for a specific, separate, root-caused failure this increment uncovers.

## DECISION — settled 2026-09-04, before the session opens

**Run this increment.** Matt: *"Do A first."* No DECISION-NEEDED remains; do not re-open one.

**The alternative was rejected on product grounds, and must not be re-proposed.** Wiring
`BarLineEstimator` on (`UZUME_BARLINE`) was offered as the cheaper first move on the strength of
FT.4.1's numbers — every beat-layer figure identical to off, take_five downbeat F 0.26 → 0.97.
Matt: **"B is not a viable option and I don't know why you would even suggest it."**

The reason it is not viable, stated so the next session does not rediscover the pitch: the estimator
**declines on 7 of 9 truthed tracks**, and a decline means no bars at all. D-205 makes meter and
downbeat a hard gate *because Nacre's and Glaze's downbeat pushes are their connection layer* — so
shipping a 7-in-9 decline removes those presets' connection to the music on most material. "Declines
rather than guesses" (D-207) is a sound property for a **metric**; it is not a shipped behaviour when
the consumer is a visual that has nothing to do when it declines. The offer was FT.4.1's engineering
framing carried forward without being converted into what the listener sees.

A future increment may revisit the estimator **only** with a materially lower decline rate — that is
FT.3.1's wrong-metrical-level question (D-210), not a wiring decision.
